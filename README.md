# On-Chain Forensics

A collection of independent on-chain investigations into token distribution, liquidity, and holder behavior. Each one starts from a question worth asking about a token, mint authority status, who actually holds supply, whether liquidity could absorb a large sell, whether a community distribution was retained or dumped, and works through it using whatever on-chain data and tooling the question actually needs.

## What's in each investigation folder

- The queries or scripts used to pull the data, kept as close as possible to what was actually run, not cleaned up after the fact to look tidier than the process was
- A methodology note stating plainly what was verified directly on-chain versus what was cited from outside reporting for context, and where the two might be confused if you're not careful
- A link to the full written report, where the findings are laid out in full

## A note on rigor

Nothing here is financial advice or a recommendation to buy, hold, or sell anything. Wallet attribution (linking an address to a real person or entity) is only ever as strong as its source, and each writeup says clearly whether that source is a signed on-chain action, a platform label, or a third-party claim. Where something couldn't be confirmed, that's stated rather than glossed over.
